# Destination & Attraction Guides — Editorial Structure

Approved reference layout, demonstrated by Mount of Olives and Mount Zion guides.

## Guiding principle

Answer visitors' practical questions in a natural order: What is this place? What should I see? How should I plan my route? When is it open? How do I get there? What else do I need to know? Only then supply broader historical context, remaining FAQs and an opportunity to contact Moshe.

Maintain a common navigation hierarchy, **not identical prose**. Each guide must reflect the destination, search intent and Moshe's firsthand, practical editorial voice.

## Section order (large heritage sites and multi-stop attractions)

0. **H1 + opening (roughly 80–120 words), local hero photograph in its natural ratio, four quick visitor facts.** No second box repeating the same trip summary. Author card can follow the essentials. The On This Page menu uses about 5–7 useful anchors and never becomes the content itself.
1. **What to See / Main Highlights.** Organize locations in a meaningful geographic or visit order. Each site card has an appropriately identified locally hosted image beside the heading and full-width descriptive text below. Cover religious/cultural significance, what to look for, and *one* distinctive practical tip. Do not embed the full transport timetable or opening hours in every card.
2. **Suggested Walking Routes / Ways to Visit.** Usually one short route and one longer route where a multi-stop itinerary genuinely exists. Include starting/ending points, approximate total visiting duration and sequenced highlights. Mention an Old City or surrounding-area combination in a brief paragraph, not by duplicating each route. Never invent distances or walk times.
3. **Opening Hours & Entrance Fees.** One compact verified reference, linked to responsible venues or trusted local visitor information. On desktop use a small table; on mobile, stacked full-width fact cards. If relevant, include a concise day-of-week note *after* the facts, not additional reworded itineraries. Opening hours/fees are subject to change.
4. **How to Get There.** Walking approach, concrete transit information and private-driver access if meaningful. Embed mapping, transport planners and walking-leg links next to the specific advice; do not display unsupported travel-time estimates.
5. **Practical Tips.** About 4–6 distinct, useful observations covering walking/terrain, accessibility, weather/light, church etiquette, services or local logistics. Avoid restating admissions, the introduction or the same steep-slope warning five times.
6. **History & Significance.** Brief and respectful background appropriate to the visitor audience, with faith traditions presented naturally. No combative archaeological or theological caveats. Keep deep historical essays separate if they would crowd out trip-planning information.
7. **Frequently Asked Questions.** Around 5–7 genuine residual visitor questions with short answers. Eliminate redundant questions that paraphrase one another or simply reproduce the preceding sections.
8. **Plan Your Visit with Moshe.** A concise, inviting introduction to own transport help; verified tour operators distinct from his service; then the existing consent-gated Brevo message form with context-specific subject and source URL. Affiliate offers only if approved and populated; never fabricate rates.
9. **Explore More Around [Location].** Usually 4 or 6 relevant local-image cards (two columns on wide screens), each with a concise description and Read more link. Use natural contextual links within the main text too.

## Compact version for smaller places

For a single gate, monument or point viewpoint, keep the first section, a focused significance/highlights passage, access & hours, 2–4 relevant practical tips, and 4–6 related destinations. Do not invent walking routes, long histories, or FAQ filler merely to complete the template.

## Design, SEO, maintenance

- Preserve stable canonical URLs and existing 301 redirect plans.
- Acknowledge Christian traditions respectfully and explain their relevance without introducing argumentative disclaimers.
- Keep headings useful, descriptive and non-repetitive; vary natural visitor advice rather than repeating "Visiting".
- Local images **only** (committed to `src/assets/` or `public/images/`); meaningful `alt`, correct subject, no unnecessary cropping and lazy load below the fold.
- Match visitor intent for the particular location. One subject belongs in one main section rather than recurring as filler.
- Internal links are woven into relevant prose and followed by the illustrated related-guide cards.
- Every operational statement (hours, fees, transit routes) should be periodically rechecked against its linked source.
- Do not ask the site visitor to contact an unavailable provider; only show approved tours or functioning contact forms.
- Verify build, strict TypeScript, unit tests, keyboard access, responsive layout, missing images, and form delivery before publishing.
